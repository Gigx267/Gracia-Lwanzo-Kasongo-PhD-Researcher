Academic Overview

I am a doctoral researcher in Political Science and International Relations at UCLouvain (Catholic University of Leuven), Belgium. My academic work sits at the intersection of public international law, international humanitarian action, and the political realities of conflict-affected regions.

My doctoral study explores how international legal norms interact with local and international humanitarian responses, examining how policy, coordination, and governance mechanisms operate during crises.

Origins & Lived Experience

Born in Goma, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), my academic perspective is deeply informed by lived experience in a region shaped by complex geopolitical dynamics and enduring humanitarian challenges.

Combining this firsthand understanding with rigorous legal and political training across Central Africa and Europe, I aim to connect abstract legal principles with the practical everyday realities of humanitarian intervention.

Research Purpose

"To advance legal-policy research that safeguards vulnerable populations and enhances governance in conflict-affected regions."

Core Pillars of Research

International Humanitarian Law

Examining legal frameworks protecting non-combatants during conflict.

Humanitarian Governance

Analyzing institutional policy, ethics, and field-level coordination.

Great Lakes Region Focus

Deep contextual analysis of political dynamics in Central Africa.

Political Science & IR

Studying state institutions, global order, and power structures.
